# Coaching inside the judge demo

**Play** is the single entry point. Older Tutorial actions open the same endless San Francisco demo. Complete the cardboard checks or choose **Use keyboard**; there is no separate sixteen-target route or sequence of guide rings.

One contextual coaching card stays visible during flight. It changes with the aircraft's real state:

- **On the ground:** increase power and pull up as speed builds.
- **Airborne with gear or flaps deployed:** press **badge A / G** to retract both.
- **Cruising:** show plasma tag ID 4 or hold Space/left mouse; release or cover to stop. New flocks keep arriving.
- **Landing mode:** press **D / badge B** to begin, configure gear and flaps with **badge A / G**, reduce throttle, and steer and pitch your own approach.
- **After touchdown:** stay on the ground and slow down. The aircraft does not bounce into another takeoff.

Live gear, flap and power readings accompany the guidance. Keyboard A is an additional gear/flap shortcut, F changes flaps alone, and comma/period steer the rudder. The coaching card replaces overlapping lesson and mission-phase cards.

The opening instructor explains yoke/throttle controls, dual plasma, automatic missile support and the electronic badge while you play. Clearing the first three geese hands off introductory narration; it does not finish the demo. The persistent context coach remains. Large flocks receive automatic bursts of four missiles, at up to one burst per second when eligible targets are available.

Narration uses local OS English speech, with music ducking and a captions-only fallback. No microphone or cloud voice service is required. Sound starts enabled; M mutes. Pause and lost-yoke recovery pause the lesson timers and narration.

Waves continue until you decide to land. The display shows elapsed time and **WAVE N**, without a countdown or fixed kill quota. Scoring only counts actual kills. Landing is a pilot-controlled approach with forgiving, planted touchdown behavior, not an automatic landing lesson.
